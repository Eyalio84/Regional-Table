method:
  - step: "Get your griddle or cast iron pan hot. Medium-high. Lay down the onion half-moons with a splash of oil. Let them go until they start to brown at the edges — 4 minutes. Don't fuss with them."

Yo — this sandwich was not invented in a restaurant. It was not plated. It was not on a menu with a wine pairing. It came out of Hajji's Deli on 110th Street in Spanish Harlem, sometime in the 1990s, from a griddle behind bulletproof glass, made by somebody who figured out that if you chop a burger apart while it's cooking and fold American cheese into the meat, you get something better than a burger. That's the whole story. No mythology required.

The bodega cat, the fluorescent light, the hot sauce in the little plastic packets, the guy behind the counter who already knows what you want before you finish ordering — that's the context. You don't need to understand it to make the sandwich, but you need to understand it to know why the *bac, egg, and cheese on a roll* ordering vernacular exists, why there are regulars who have eaten this exact sandwich from this exact bodega every morning for fifteen years, and why it became the subject of a documentary before Manhattan even noticed it existed.

If you are reading this from somewhere with a Whole Foods within walking distance and you are thinking about substituting the Goya sazon with something from the spice aisle and the American cheese with something that comes from an actual farm — don't. Make that different sandwich and call it something else. This one requires the yellow can and the individually wrapped slice. That's not nostalgia. That's the recipe.
